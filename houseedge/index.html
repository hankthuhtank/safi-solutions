The one number to know

The house edge is a price tag, not a prediction.

Every casino bet pays a little less than its true odds. That gap is the house edge: the percentage of each bet the casino keeps on average, over a very large number of bets.

Worked example · a single number in roulette

  1. A single-zero wheel has37 pockets
  2. A straight-up bet pays35 to 1
  3. A fair price would be36 to 1
  4. Bet $1 on all 37 pockets: you win onceget back $36
  5. You spent $37, got $36 back−$1 ÷ $37 = 2.70%

Tonight can go either way — short-run results swing far more than the edge. But the edge is what the casino collects across millions of bets, and it's why a longer or faster session costs more.

How a hand works

  1. Bet, then two cards each. Players get two face-up cards; the dealer gets one up and one face down (the "hole card").
  2. Card values. 2–10 count face value, J/Q/K count 10, an ace counts 1 or 11. A hand with an ace counted as 11 is "soft".
  3. Your choices. Hit (take a card), stand, double (double the bet, take exactly one card), split a pair into two hands, or surrender half the bet where offered.
  4. The dealer has no choices. The dealer must draw to 16 and stand on 17 — the rule printed on the felt says whether a soft 17 is hit.
  5. Payouts. A win pays 1:1, a natural (ace + ten-value card) pays 3:2, a tie is a "push" and your bet stays.

Table 02 Baccarat

Most of baccarat is automatic. Your only decision is the bet.

Two hands are dealt — "Player" and "Banker". Neither belongs to you or the casino; they're just names. You bet on which hand finishes closest to 9, and the dealer draws every card by fixed rules.

Counting a hand

  • Ace = 1, 2–9 = face value, 10/J/Q/K = 0.
  • Only the last digit counts. 7 + 8 = 15, so the hand is worth 5.
  • A natural ends it. If either hand's first two cards total 8 or 9, nobody draws.
  • Player draws on 0–5 and stands on 6–7.
  • Banker's draw depends on Player's third card — use the tableau to see every case.

No-commission tables (e.g. EZ Baccarat) push a winning Banker three-card 7 instead of taking 5%; Banker's edge there is about 1.02%. Some tables pay 9:1 on Tie, which cuts that bet's edge to about 4.84%.

Scoreboards aren't forecasts. The "roadmap" screens that track streaks of Banker and Player wins describe the past. Each hand is dealt from the shoe independently of the last one.

Why 7 rules the game

Two dice make 36 equally likely combinations. Six of them add to 7 — more than any other total.

    Rolls: 0

    The round, in three steps

    1. Come-out roll. Pass line wins on 7 or 11 ("natural") and loses on 2, 3 or 12 ("craps").
    2. A point is set. Any 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10 becomes the point, and the dealer flips the puck to ON over that number.
    3. Point or seven. Roll the point again before a 7 and the pass line wins. A 7 first ("seven-out") loses, and the dice pass to the next shooter.

    Taking odds

    Once a point is set you can add an odds bet behind your pass line. It pays exactly the true odds, so it carries no house edge at all.

      Floor 05 Slots

      RTP is the long-run average. Volatility is how the ride feels.

      A slot's outcome is chosen by a random number generator the instant you press spin; the reels just display it. Nothing you do changes the odds — but the machine's math decides how fast money leaves.

      Read a machine like a spec sheet

      RTP · return to player
      The share of all money wagered that a machine pays back over millions of spins. 92% RTP = an 8% edge. It says nothing about your next hour.
      Volatility
      How the return is packaged: many small wins (low) or rare big ones (high). Same RTP, very different sessions.
      Hit frequency
      How often a spin pays anything — including "wins" smaller than your bet.
      Bet per spin
      A “penny” machine rarely bets a penny — most spins cost dozens of credits. Check the total bet on screen, not the denomination.
      Speed
      About 600 spins an hour is typical. That's why a small edge on slots costs more per hour than a bigger one at a slow table.

      The part that matters most

      Better strategy lowers the price. It can't make gambling predictable.

      1. Decide the budget before you walk in — and treat it as the cost of entertainment, like a concert ticket.
      2. Set a time limit too. The average cost grows with every hour and every bet.
      3. Never chase losses. Raising bets to "win it back" raises the average cost and the size of the swings.
      4. No system beats a negative edge. Progressions like the Martingale change how you lose, not whether.
      5. Don't borrow to gamble, and don't gamble to fix money problems.

      Glossary

      Twelve words that unlock the casino.

      House edge
      The average share of each bet the casino keeps over the long run.
      RTP
      Return to player — the flip side of the edge. 95% RTP means a 5% edge.
      Variance
      How far short-term results swing around the average.
      Push
      A tie. Your bet is returned, nothing won or lost.
      Natural
      A winning total on the first two cards: 21 in blackjack, 8 or 9 in baccarat.
      Soft hand
      A blackjack hand with an ace counted as 11 — it can't bust on the next card.
      Point
      In craps, the number (4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10) the shooter must repeat before a 7.
      True odds
      The payout that would make a bet perfectly fair. Casino payouts are set below it.
      Vig / juice
      The sportsbook's built-in margin, e.g. risking $110 to win $100.
      Implied probability
      The win chance a price assumes. −110 implies 52.38%.
      Outs
      In poker, the unseen cards that would improve your hand to a likely winner.
      Rake
      The fee a poker room takes from each pot.
