1ST · The basics

Start here: the words

Read this first. These are the words the rest of the page assumes you know.

Core vocabulary22 words used everywhere below

Inning

A segment of the game in which each team gets a turn to bat. An MLB game is normally nine innings.

Out

One of the three retirements the defense needs to end the batting team’s half-inning.

Plate appearance

One complete turn at the plate, ending when the batter reaches base, makes an out, or another result ends the turn.

At-bat

A statistical type of plate appearance. Some plate appearances, such as walks, do not count as official at-bats.

Count

The number of balls and strikes on the current batter, written balls first: 3-1 means three balls and one strike.

Strike zone

The area over home plate where a pitch can be called a strike when it meets the rulebook height limits.

Ball

A pitch outside the strike zone that the batter does not swing at.

Strike

A pitch in the strike zone, a swing and miss, or most foul balls before two strikes.

Force play

A situation where a runner must advance because the batter became a runner and is pushing the runners ahead.

Tag play

A runner is out when a fielder holding the ball touches the runner before the runner reaches a safe base, when no force applies.

Fair ball

A batted ball that is in fair territory under the rules and remains live unless another rule stops play.

Foul ball

A batted ball that ends up in foul territory under the rules. Most foul balls count as a strike unless the batter already has two strikes.

Pitch

The ball thrown by the pitcher toward home plate.

Pitch type

A category of pitch such as fastball, slider, curveball, or changeup, each using different speed and movement.

Bullpen

The group of relief pitchers who enter after the starting pitcher or in specialized late-game roles.

Starter

The pitcher who begins the game for a team.

Reliever

A pitcher who enters after the starter.

Cutoff / relay

A fielder used as an intermediate target so an outfielder can move the ball efficiently toward a base or home plate.

Tag up

After a caught fly ball, a runner must retouch the starting base before trying to advance.

Steal

A runner tries to advance during the pitch without waiting for the batter to hit the ball.

Platoon advantage

Using a hitter or pitcher matchup based partly on handedness, because left/right matchups can affect performance.

Run

A point scored when a runner legally touches first, second, third, and home plate in order.

1ST · The basics

The game at a glance

Nine innings; each has a top (the visitors bat) and a bottom (the home team bats). Three outs end a half-inning. There is no clock on the game itself — but since 2023 there is one on the pitcher.

Inning

Each team gets a turn to bat. The visiting team bats in the top half; home team in the bottom half.

Out

One of three retirements that end a half-inning.

Run

A score, completed when a runner legally touches first, second, third, and home.

At-bat

A plate appearance that ends in most standard batting results; walks and some other events are plate appearances but not at-bats for statistics.

Ball

Pitch outside the strike zone that the batter does not swing at. Four balls award first base.

Strike

Pitch in the zone not swung at, a swing-and-miss, or certain fouls. Three strikes retire the batter.

Foul ball

Batted ball outside fair territory under the rules. With fewer than two strikes, most fouls add a strike; ordinary foul balls do not create strike three.

Hit

Batter reaches safely on fair batted ball without an error/fielder’s choice being the reason.

Walk (BB)

Four balls award first base.

Hit by pitch (HBP)

Batter is struck by a pitch under applicable rules and is generally awarded first base.

Home run

Fair batted ball leaves the park in play or otherwise qualifies, allowing batter and runners to score.

Extra innings

Played when the game remains tied after regulation.

How the game runsRules that shape every inning
  • A standard MLB game is scheduled for nine innings.
  • Three outs end each half-inning.
  • The batting order cycles continuously from one inning to the next.
  • Pitch timer (since 2023): 15 seconds with the bases empty, 18 with runners on. The batter must be set and alert with 8 seconds left. A pitcher violation is an automatic ball; a batter violation an automatic strike.
  • Disengagements: a pitcher may step off or throw over only twice per plate appearance with runners on. A third, unsuccessful pickoff is a balk — every runner moves up a base.
  • Extra innings: each half-inning after the ninth starts with a runner on second base.
  • Designated hitter: both leagues have used the DH since 2022, so pitchers rarely bat in MLB.

1ST · The basics

Field and defensive positions

Every fielder has a number used in scorekeeping. A “6-4-3 double play” means shortstop to second baseman to first baseman.

Pitcher (1)

Delivers pitches, fields the center of the diamond, controls running game, and executes pickoffs.

Catcher (2)

Receives pitches, blocks balls, controls running game, communicates signs/strategy, and directs much of the defense.

First baseman (3)

Handles many throws, holds runners, and fields right side.

Second baseman (4)

Middle infielder with range; turns double plays and covers bases.

Third baseman (5)

Handles hard-hit balls and bunts near third; long throw across diamond.

Shortstop (6)

Premium range/arm position; covers left side and anchors many double plays.

Left fielder (7)

Outfielder responsible for left field; backs up throws and reads caroms.

Center fielder (8)

Usually widest outfield range; often takes priority on fly balls.

Right fielder (9)

Outfielder in right; often requires strong arm for throws to third/home.

Designated hitter (DH)

Bats in lineup without playing a defensive position under DH rules.

Cutoff man

Fielder positioned to relay throws from outfield to an appropriate base.

Backing up

Moving behind a teammate/base to stop an overthrow from becoming extra bases.

4TH · Pitcher vs. hitter

The count and plate-appearance strategy

The count — balls first, then strikes — is the single best summary of who is winning the at-bat. Hitters hunt in their counts; pitchers expand the zone in theirs.

0-0

Neutral count. Pitcher wants strike one; hitter decides whether to hunt a specific pitch.

0-1 / 0-2

Pitcher ahead. Hitter protects more of the zone and must respect chase pitches.

1-0 / 2-0 / 3-1

Hitter-favorable counts. Fastball or strike-zone predictability may rise, and hitter can hunt a location.

2-1

Balanced but hitter-friendly. Both sides can still access broad arsenals.

2-2

Battle count. Pitcher can expand; hitter must cover zone without chasing everything.

3-2 full count

Next qualifying pitch decides walk or strikeout unless fouled off. Runners may move on the pitch in some situations.

Protecting the plate

With two strikes, hitter becomes more defensive and prioritizes contact on borderline strikes.

Working the count

Taking/selecting pitches to gain information, reach hitter-favorable counts, or raise pitch count.

Ambush hitting

Attacking an early pitch because it matches a pre-planned zone/pitch expectation.

Two-strike approach

Shortening swing / expanding coverage / prioritizing contact, depending on hitter philosophy.

4TH · Pitcher vs. hitter

Pitch types and what they do

Pitches differ in speed, spin and movement. Movement is measured against a spinless ball: a four-seamer “rises” only compared with how far it would have dropped.

Four-seam fastball

Usually highest-velocity fastball, often with “ride” or less vertical drop than expected.

Two-seam fastball

Fastball with more arm-side movement/sink depending on pitcher.

Sinker

Fastball variant emphasizing downward and arm-side movement to generate ground balls.

Cutter

Fastball-like pitch with late glove-side movement, smaller than a slider.

Slider

Breaking ball with glove-side and downward movement; speed/movement vary widely.

Sweeper

Slider family with pronounced horizontal glove-side movement.

Curveball

Breaking ball with larger downward component and generally more speed separation than a slider.

Knuckle curve

Curve family thrown with a knuckle/finger-grip variation; not the same as a knuckleball.

Changeup

Thrown with fastball arm action but lower velocity; relies on timing disruption and often arm-side fade.

Circle change

Common changeup grip/type often featuring arm-side movement.

Splitter

Fastball-like release that drops sharply late due to spin/velocity characteristics.

Forkball

Splitter relative with deeper split grip and often more drop/lower velocity.

Knuckleball

Very low-spin pitch with unpredictable movement; rare in modern MLB.

Eephus

Very slow, high-arcing surprise pitch.

The art of itSequencing and tunneling
  • Pitching is sequencing: speed, location, movement, eye level, and tunneling matter together.
  • A great slider is more effective when the hitter must also respect a fastball from a similar release window.
  • Pitchers use “waste” pitches or chase pitches outside the zone when ahead, but falling behind creates walk risk.

4TH · Pitcher vs. hitter

Hitting concepts

Modern hitting is measured at contact: how hard (exit velocity) and at what angle (launch angle). Hard contact in the right window becomes a barrel.

Contact

Putting the ball in play. Contact quality matters: weak contact can still produce outs.

Exit velocity

Speed of the batted ball immediately after contact.

Launch angle

Vertical angle of the batted ball.

Barrel

Statcast quality-of-contact classification combining exit velocity and launch angle.

Pull

Hit toward batter’s pull side.

Opposite field

Hit to the field opposite batter’s natural pull side.

Gap power

Ability to drive balls into spaces between outfielders for doubles/triples.

Plate discipline

Swinging at hittable pitches and taking pitches outside the zone.

Chase rate

How often a hitter swings at pitches outside the strike zone.

Zone rate

Share of pitches thrown in the strike zone.

Sacrifice fly

Fly ball that results in a runner scoring after a catch and meets scoring criteria.

Bunt

Intentional deadened contact used for placement, sacrifice, surprise, or squeeze situations.

Hit-and-run

Runner goes on pitch while batter is expected to swing/contact, opening defensive holes and protecting runner.

Run-and-hit

Runner goes, but hitter retains more discretion whether to swing.

7TH · Defense & baserunning

Baserunning

Runners advance on hits, walks, steals and outs. Whether a runner is forced to run decides how the defense can get him out.

Lead

Distance a runner takes off a base before the pitch.

Secondary lead

Additional movement as pitch reaches plate to improve reaction/start.

Stolen base

Runner advances during pitch without benefit of hit/error under scoring rules.

Caught stealing

Runner is put out attempting to steal.

Pickoff

Pitcher/catcher throws to a base to retire or shorten a runner’s lead.

Tag up

Runner retouches original base after a caught fly ball before attempting to advance.

Force play

Runner must advance because batter becomes runner and occupies the base behind.

Tag play

Fielder must physically tag runner (or meet specific base/appeal conditions) because no force exists.

Rundown

Defense traps runner between bases and exchanges throws to apply a tag.

First-to-third

Runner on first tries to reach third on a single, depending on ball location, speed, and arm strength.

Scoring from second

Runner reads ball/outs and attempts to score on a single.

Infield in

Fielders move closer to plate to improve chance of throwing out runner at home; increases risk of grounders getting through.

7TH · Defense & baserunning

Team defense, alignments and relays

Fielders reposition on almost every pitch — for the hitter, the count, the runners and the score. Since 2023 there are limits on how the infield can shift.

Double-play depth

Middle infielders position to turn two quickly.

Corners in

First/third baseman move closer for bunt defense or runner-at-third situations.

No-doubles defense

Outfielders play deeper to prevent extra-base hits late with a lead.

Outfield shift

Outfielders move based on hitter spray tendencies, pitch plan, and game situation.

Infield positioning restrictions

Since 2023: when the pitch is released, all four infielders must have both feet on the infield dirt, with two on each side of second base. Infielders cannot switch sides unless there is a substitution.

Cutoff

Relay throw is directed through a cutoff fielder to control runners and improve accuracy.

Relay

Long throw chain from outfield toward a distant base/home.

Priority

Center fielder generally has priority on many outfield fly balls; communication prevents collisions.

Backup responsibility

Non-primary fielders move behind likely throw destination to contain errors.

Bunt defense

Corners charge; middle infield/pitcher/catcher rotate to cover bases depending on runners.

First-and-third defense

Special plays designed to control a stealing runner while preventing runner on third from scoring.

Wheel play

Aggressive bunt defense with corner infielders charging and middle infielders rotating.

7TH · Defense & baserunning

Pitching strategy and bullpen roles

Starters give length; relievers give leverage. Managers balance fatigue, handedness and how many times a hitter has already seen a pitcher.

Starter

Begins game and aims to provide multiple innings while navigating lineup repeatedly.

Opener

Short first pitcher used strategically before bulk innings from another pitcher.

Bulk reliever

Pitcher expected to cover multiple middle innings.

Setup reliever

High-leverage reliever used before closer, traditionally eighth inning.

Closer

Reliever commonly used to protect late leads, often in ninth.

Long reliever

Covers several innings when starter exits early or game goes long.

Left/right matchup

Pitcher-batter handedness can change pitch movement and platoon advantage.

Times through the order

Hitters often improve after seeing a pitcher multiple times; managers weigh this against bullpen quality.

Pitch count

Workload metric affecting fatigue and removal decisions.

Pitch sequencing

Ordering pitches to alter speed, eye level, location, and hitter expectation.

Tunneling

Different pitches travel similarly early before separating late.

Pitching backward

Using off-speed/breaking pitches in counts where fastball is traditionally expected.

Three-batter minimum

Since 2020 a reliever must face at least three batters (or finish the half-inning) unless injured — which ended the one-batter lefty specialist.

10TH · Strategy & stats

Situational strategy

There are exactly 24 base-out states — eight ways to arrange runners times zero, one or two outs. Each has a measured average number of runs that follow. That table explains modern strategy.

Runner on 3rd, less than 2 outs

Offense values any ball in play that can score runner; defense may play in.

Runner on 2nd, no outs

Offense may prioritize advancing runner; modern strategy varies by hitter quality.

Bases loaded

Force exists at every base. Walk/HBP scores a run.

Two outs

Runners can move aggressively on contact because a caught ball ends inning anyway.

Late lead

Defense prioritizes preventing extra bases; bullpen leverage increases.

Tie game late

Managers may emphasize matchup, bullpen, pinch runners, and one-run tactics.

Squeeze play

Bunt attempt designed specifically to score runner from third.

Intentional walk

Defense awards first base intentionally to change matchup or create force situation.

Pinch hitter

Substitute batter used for matchup or offense; removed player cannot return under MLB rules.

Pinch runner

Substitute runner used for speed or baserunning; replaces original player.

Defensive replacement

Better fielder enters late to protect lead.

Sacrifice bunt

Batter gives up likely out to advance runner; value depends heavily on context and run expectancy.

10TH · Strategy & stats

Statistics you actually need

Traditional stats count events; modern stats weight them by value and adjust for ballpark and era. The first three below are the foundation.

AVG

Batting average: hits divided by at-bats.

OBP

On-base percentage: (hits + walks + hit-by-pitch) ÷ (at-bats + walks + hit-by-pitch + sacrifice flies).

SLG

Slugging percentage: total bases ÷ at-bats (single = 1, double = 2, triple = 3, home run = 4).

OPS

OBP + SLG; simple combined offensive measure.

OPS+

League/park-adjusted OPS metric where 100 is league average.

wRC+

Comprehensive run-creation rate adjusted to league/park; 100 is average.

HR

Home runs.

RBI

Runs batted in; context-dependent counting stat.

SB

Stolen bases.

ERA

Earned runs allowed per nine innings: earned runs × 9 ÷ innings pitched.

WHIP

Walks plus hits allowed per inning pitched.

K/9

Strikeouts per nine innings.

BB/9

Walks per nine innings.

FIP

Fielding Independent Pitching; estimates outcomes tied more directly to pitcher: strikeouts, walks, HBP, HR.

WAR

Wins Above Replacement estimate combining contributions into one value; implementations vary.

BABIP

Batting average on balls in play, excluding HR and strikeouts from denominator structure.

ISO

Isolated power: SLG minus AVG.

Hard-hit rate

Share of batted balls above Statcast hard-hit threshold.

xBA / xSLG / xwOBA

Expected metrics based on quality/contact characteristics and other inputs.

Save

Relief pitching statistic awarded under specific qualifying conditions.

Hold

Credited to a reliever who enters in a save situation and leaves with the lead intact. Widely tracked, but not an official MLB statistic.

10TH · Strategy & stats

MLB The Show and baseball-game mode

The same count, sequencing and base-out logic wins in video games.

Play smarterTransferable habits
  • Learn count leverage first. When ahead as pitcher, expand; when behind, prioritize strikes without becoming predictable.
  • As a hitter, avoid chasing. Waiting for your pitch is usually more valuable than swinging at everything in the zone’s neighborhood.
  • Sequence velocity and movement: hard inside can make soft away play up; breaking down/away can set up fastball above the zone.
  • Know where double play exists. With runner on first and fewer than two outs, a ground ball can become two outs.
  • Use defensive positioning and cutoff logic—throwing directly to home is not always the right play.
  • Baserunning mistakes are costly. Take the extra base only when ball location/arm situation gives a real edge.
  • Manage bullpen by leverage, handedness, stamina, and who is due up—not only by inning labels.

Video-game interfaces and pitch ratings change by title/year. The goal is to teach transferable baseball logic.

Rulebook watch

New for 2026

MLB’s rules have changed more since 2023 than in the previous fifty years. Here is what is current for the 2026 season.

2026 season

ABS challenge system in every game

Umpires still call balls and strikes, but the batter, pitcher or catcher can challenge immediately by tapping the helmet or cap — no help from the dugout. Each team starts with two challenges and keeps any it wins; after two failed challenges it has none left. A team out of challenges gets one for each extra inning. The replay is shown on the videoboard.

2026 season

A strike zone set by height

For ABS the zone top is 53.5% of the batter’s standing height and the bottom 27%, across the 17-inch plate. A pitch is a strike if any part of the ball touches that zone.

Since 2024

Pitch timer 15 / 18 seconds

15 seconds with the bases empty and 18 with runners on (it was 20 with runners in 2023). Violations are an automatic ball or strike.

Since 2023

Shift limits and bigger bases

Two infielders on each side of second base, all four on the dirt. First, second and third base grew from 15 to 18 inches square, shortening the distance between bases.

Since 2023

Two disengagements per plate appearance

Step-offs and pickoff throws are limited. A third unsuccessful attempt is a balk.

Baseball glossary

Every term from this page in plain English. Dotted-underlined words anywhere above also explain themselves when you hover or tap them.

Ace
Team’s top starting pitcher.
Around the horn
Infield throw sequence or phrase for 5-4-3 double play style.
Automatic runner
The runner placed on second base to start each extra-inning half-inning.
Backdoor breaking ball
Breaking pitch starts outside and moves back over edge.
Backstop
Barrier behind home plate; sometimes colloquial for catcher.
Balk
Illegal pitcher action with runners on base resulting in penalty under rules.
Barrel
High-quality batted-ball classification.
Base-out state
The combination of which bases are occupied and how many outs there are — 24 in all.
Batting order
Fixed hitter sequence.
Bleeder
Weakly hit ball that finds a gap.
Bullpen
Relief pitchers and the warming area.
Can of corn
Easy fly ball.
Check swing

Cleanup hitter
Traditional fourth hitter.
Cycle
Single, double, triple, HR in same game.
Dinger
Home run.
Disengagement
A pitcher stepping off the rubber or attempting a pickoff. Limited to two per plate appearance.
Double play
Two outs on one continuous play.
Dugout
Team bench area.
Error
Fielder misplay judged to allow advancement that ordinary effort would have prevented.

Double, triple, or HR.
Fielder’s choice

Frozen rope

Grand slam
Home run with bases loaded.
Green light
Permission/encouragement to swing or steal in situation.
Ground-rule double
Awarded two bases under ballpark/universal ground rules.
Hit-and-run
Runner goes and batter swings.
Hold runner
Pitcher varies looks/timing to limit steal.
In the hole
Deep fielding area or upcoming hitter phrase depending context.
Induced vertical break
How much a pitch rises or drops compared with a spinless ball thrown at the same speed and angle.
Infield fly
Rule protecting runners from intentional dropped easy pop with force setup.
Launch angle
Vertical angle of batted ball.
LOOGY
Historic slang for a left-handed specialist reliever; the three-batter minimum (2020) ended the role.
Mendoza Line
Informal .200 batting-average reference.
No-hitter
No hits allowed by opposing team over qualifying game.
Passed ball
Catchable pitch not handled by catcher, allowing advancement.
Perfect game
No opposing batter reaches base in complete game.
Pitch timer
Clock limiting time between pitches: 15 seconds with bases empty, 18 with runners on.
Pitchout
Deliberate pitch away from hitter to help catcher throw out runner.

Use players based on handedness matchup.
Quality start

RBI

Rubber

Sac fly

Save situation
Game context that can qualify reliever for save.
Shift

Small ball
Run-manufacturing style using bunts, steals, contact, advancement.
Southpaw
Left-handed pitcher.
Strike zone
Space over plate defined by rules and batter stance.
Tag
Touch runner with ball/glove containing ball when tag required.
Take
Do not swing.
Texas leaguer
Soft blooper between infield/outfield.
Walk-off
Game-ending hit/walk/etc. by home team taking lead in final inning/extra innings.
Warning track
Track near outfield wall.
Wild pitch
Pitch judged too difficult for ordinary catcher control, allowing advancement.
