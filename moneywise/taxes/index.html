Part IWhat it is

Federal income tax is figured on a ladder. Your income fills the lowest bracket first, then the next one up, and only the dollars inside each bracket pay that bracket's rate. Being "in the 22% bracket" means your last dollars are taxed at 22%, not all of them.

Before the ladder, income shrinks in two steps. Pre-tax deductions from your paycheck (a traditional 401(k), an HSA, health premiums) never show up as taxable wages. Then the standard deduction (or your itemized deductions) comes off. What is left is taxable income, and that is what the brackets apply to.

A second tax on every paycheck

Social Security and Medicare come out separately: 6.2% of wages up to the wage base ($184,500 in 2026) and 1.45% of all wages, plus 0.9% above $200,000 for single filers. Traditional 401(k) contributions do not reduce them.

Part IIHow it works

Brackets are buckets.

Picture income pouring into a row of buckets. The 10% bucket fills first, then the 12% bucket, and so on. Each bucket's rate applies only to what is in it. Here is the worked example below: a single filer with $75,000 of salary and $54,400 of taxable income.

Marginal versus effective

Your marginal rate is the rate on your next dollar (22% here). Your effective rate is your total tax divided by your income (8.9% of salary here). Use the marginal rate to judge a raise, overtime or a deduction; use the effective rate to understand your overall bill.

Deduction or credit?

A $1,000 deduction lowers taxable income, so in the 22% bracket it saves $220. A $1,000 credit lowers the tax itself and saves $1,000. A refundable credit can even be paid to you.

New deductions for 2025 to 2028

The 2025 tax law added four deductions you can take even with the standard deduction, each on Schedule 1-A and each shrinking at higher incomes. Married couples must file jointly to claim them.

Part VCommon mistakes

  1. Error

    Fearing a raise will push you into a higher bracket

    Only the dollars above the line pay the higher rate. In the example, the next $1,000 of pay still leaves $704 after federal income and payroll taxes.

    The fixTake the raise, the overtime or the bonus. (A few benefit programs do have cliffs; check those separately.)

  2. Error

    Treating a deduction like a credit

    A $1,000 deduction saves $100 to $370 depending on your bracket. A $1,000 credit saves $1,000.

    The fixValue a deduction at your marginal rate before you spend money to get one.

  3. Error

    Celebrating a big refund

    The IRS reported a $3,276 average refund through May 8, 2026. That is $126 every two weeks you lent the government at 0%.

    The fixUse the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator and update your W-4 so more of it lands in each paycheck.

  4. Error

    Not updating your W-4 after life changes

    Marriage, a second job, a spouse's job or a new child can all leave you badly over- or under-withheld.

    The fixRevisit your W-4 after every big change, and at least once a year.

  5. Error

    Assuming a 401(k) cuts every tax

    Traditional contributions lower income tax, but Social Security and Medicare still come out of every dollar you earn.

    The fixCount the 7.65% when you estimate what a contribution really costs your paycheck.

  6. Error

    Forgetting taxes on side income

    Freelance and gig income has no withholding, and owes self-employment tax (15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings) on top of income tax.

    The fixSet aside about a third of side income and pay estimated taxes quarterly to avoid penalties.

  7. Error

    Missing the new deductions, or claiming them wrongly

    Qualified tips, the overtime premium and new-car loan interest can be deducted for 2025 to 2028 without itemizing, but only the extra "half" of overtime counts, and married couples must file jointly.

    The fixKeep your W-2, 1099 and loan statements, and fill out Schedule 1-A line by line.

  8. Error

    Selling investments without checking the 0% bracket

    Long-term capital gains are taxed at 0% while taxable income stays under $49,450 (single) in 2026.

    The fixIn a low-income year, consider taking gains up to that line tax-free.
