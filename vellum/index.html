An apparatus for reading the Bible as literature

Long before modern printed editions, biblical texts circulated as scrolls and codices, copied by hand on papyrus and parchment, with readers and scribes leaving notes around the text. Vellum turns that same instinct into an interactive desk: text, places, people, cross-references, sourced notes, and a guided path through the larger story.

Sources stay visible

Scripture, commentary, people data, cross-references, and geographic coordinates are tied to named open sources. Vellum's Chronicle hooks, interface copy, diagrams, and study questions are editorial layers built on top of those sources, and the Colophon tells you which is which.

Close-reading notebook Observe · Interpret · Revisit

Start with what the passage actually says. Keep your interpretation separate from the evidence and from questions still open.

A worked example from Luke 10

Observation: verse 29 asks who the speaker’s neighbor is; verse 36 asks which traveler acted as a neighbor. Verse 37 identifies the one who showed mercy.

Interpretation: the change in question directs attention toward action. That is a reading supported by the passage, rather than a quotation or the only possible theological interpretation.

Revisit: how do verses 25–28 shape the exchange? What do different translations make clearer?

Unrolling

Chronicle

Forty-eight selected passages in canonical order, gathered into six quires. This is a guided narrative spine rather than a substitute for the full text. Read these first and the larger sequence becomes easier to follow. Click a folio to open that passage in the reading desk. Pass its collation in the Scriptorium and the folio turns gold.

Atlas

Almost everything happens in a strip of land you could drive across in a day. The places are drawn the way a medieval cartographer drew them (walled towns and scalloped hills) but sat on real survey coordinates from OpenBible.info. Drag with one finger to pan. Use the + / − buttons to zoom (or pinch on some devices). Double-tap to zoom in. Pick any name from the index to fly to it.

Register

Every named person in the Bible, 3,067 of them, not a curated few. Each carries their family links, how many verses name them, and Easton's 1897 dictionary entry where one exists. Click a name to open the entry; blue text inside it opens the passage; family names are clickable too, so you can walk a bloodline from Adam down.

Threads

The book quotes and answers itself constantly. This draws the 344,799-link cross-reference web out from whatever verse you pick. Thicker line and closer to the centre means a stronger link, green is the Hebrew Bible, blue is the New Testament. Click any node to go read it.

Scriptorium

Use the Scriptorium as retrieval practice. Manuscript collation meant checking a copy against an exemplar; here the metaphor becomes an eight-question review built from the passage itself. Choose any leaf in the Great Quire: six correct answers out of eight gilds that passage for future sessions.

Colophon

A colophon is the note a scribe left at the end saying who copied the book and from what. This is Vellum's source ledger: scripture and linked study datasets come from the projects below; the Chronicle framing, interface text, diagrams, and practice layer are Vellum's own editorial work.
